Bitcoin drops below $69,000 as market jitters deepen

Bitcoin fell to $68,408 on Friday as forced crypto selling followed a break below $70,000, while traders weighed Nvidia’s leadership shock and a possible Fed hike.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 16, 2026 · 03:10 UTC2 min read

Bitcoin fell to $68,408 early Friday, its lowest price since Aug. 20, as traders weighed Nvidia’s leadership shake-up and expectations of a Federal Reserve rate hike. The Block reported the price drop and crypto liquidations.

The drop accelerated after bitcoin slipped below $70,000 during Asian trading, according to The Block. Analysts said the break forced traders with leveraged long positions—bets that prices would rise—to sell. Those sales added to the downward pressure.

Forced selling amplified the fall

Crypto liquidations totaled $1.47 billion over the 24 hours covered by The Block’s report, citing CoinGlass data. Of that, $1.31 billion came from long positions. In the four hours before the report, liquidations reached $798 million, including $718.5 million in longs.

Rick Maeda, head of markets at Laevitas, told The Block that bitcoin had already fallen below $72,000 in the U.S. session. When it later broke $70,000 in Asia, clustered long positions were forced out quickly, he said. Liquidations happen when exchanges close traders’ positions after they can no longer meet margin requirements.

Nvidia and rate fears weighed on sentiment

The fall came as traders assessed Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s resignation and the prospect of higher U.S. interest rates. The Associated Press reported that Nvidia shares fell after Huang’s departure. Nvidia is a major technology company, and its stock decline added to worries about the wider AI trade.

Traders were also anticipating a possible Fed rate increase in December, The Block reported. Higher rates can make riskier assets less attractive to investors. BTSE chief operating officer Jeff Mei told the outlet that traders were watching for signs of a possible half-point increase, which would be larger than the quarter-point move he described as expected.

Bitcoin had already been under pressure

The decline followed several days of selling in bitcoin and other crypto assets. CoinDesk reported on Thursday that U.S. spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds had lost $299.9 million on Wednesday, marking five straight days of outflows. An exchange-traded fund, or ETF, lets investors buy exposure to an asset through a fund traded on a stock exchange.

Those outflows and the forced liquidations added selling pressure as investors reacted to Nvidia news and changing rate expectations. The Block’s report described both as factors weighing on sentiment; it did not establish that either one alone caused bitcoin’s fall.
