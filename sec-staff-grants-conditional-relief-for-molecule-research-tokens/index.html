SEC staff grants conditional relief for Molecule research tokens

SEC staff said it would not recommend enforcement over LabsCo’s proposed research tokens on stated terms, while stressing its response rests on those facts.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 26, 2026 · 18:24 UTC2 min read

The SEC’s Division of Corporation Finance said on Nov. 24 it would not recommend enforcement action over a proposed sale of Molecule research tokens, if the sale follows the terms presented by the company’s lawyers. The SEC’s response to Molecule AG covers its US subsidiary, LabsCo, and two registration rules: Section 5 of the Securities Act and Section 12(g) of the Exchange Act.

The response is limited. The SEC said it relies on the facts and representations in Molecule’s request, and that different facts could lead to a different position. It also said the letter gives the division’s enforcement position only, not a legal conclusion about the tokens.

Tokens offer research access and advisory votes

Molecule’s lawyers described Project Tokens as a way to access and participate in scientific research. According to crypto.news’ account of the request, one token would provide membership in a project’s community and access to basic features, such as a research dashboard or project records.

Holders could also choose to lock tokens to take part in research polls. Each locked token would count as one vote when LabsCo seeks input on priorities or research direction. Those votes are advisory: LabsCo would decide whether to hold a poll and whether to follow its result.

The proposed tokens would not represent shares or give holders a claim on LabsCo’s profits. A later share sale, if LabsCo chooses to pursue one, would be a separate offering. People could apply without holding or locking Project Tokens, and token ownership would give no priority for an allocation.

The relief depends on the proposed terms

The request says LabsCo would publish a white paper before any token sale, describing the project, token uses, distribution policy and risks. It also says the company would not promise token price gains or returns. Unlocked tokens could trade on secondary markets, but the request makes no promise of a particular level of liquidity.

For readers, the key limit is that the letter does not set a general rule for research tokens. It addresses LabsCo’s proposed sales under the specific conditions described to SEC staff. The project’s published terms and marketing will show whether the offering follows those conditions.
