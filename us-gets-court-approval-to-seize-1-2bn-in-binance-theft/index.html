US gets court approval to seize $1.2bn in Binance theft

The Justice Department says a court authorized it to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in crypto stolen from Binance, as recovery efforts continue.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 21, 2026 · 16:10 UTC2 min read

US authorities have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a major step in the effort to recover funds from a $6 billion theft. The Justice Department announcement says the court action covers part of the stolen assets already frozen by US agencies and private companies.

The department says members of North Korea’s military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or around October 29. The FBI had attributed the attack to North Korea in an earlier alert. The loss is large, but the latest action does not mean the full amount has been recovered.

More than $2.7 billion has been frozen

Since the theft, US government agencies and private sector partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion in assets, according to the Justice Department. The newly authorized amount — over $1.2 billion — is part of that total. “Seize or restrain” means authorities can take control of assets or legally prevent them from moving while the case proceeds.

The department says it worked with virtual asset service providers, the companies that handle crypto transactions and custody, to stop the funds moving beyond law enforcement’s reach. That cooperation matters because cryptocurrency can move between services and across blockchains quickly. The department says the effort to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets is still under way.

Funds remain in a legal process

Some of the stolen assets are still being laundered through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges, the Justice Department says. Bridges move assets between blockchains; mixers pool transactions to make their paths harder to follow. The FBI has urged virtual asset service providers to block transactions linked to addresses used to launder the Binance funds.

Court authorization is a step toward taking control of the funds, but it does not by itself settle who will ultimately receive them. The Associated Press report notes that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered assets. The Justice Department says its work to trace and forfeit the Binance funds continues.
