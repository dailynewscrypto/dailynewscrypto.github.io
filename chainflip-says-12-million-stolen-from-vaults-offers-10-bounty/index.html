Chainflip says $12 million stolen from vaults, offers 10% bounty

Chainflip says an attacker took bitcoin, ether and stablecoins worth about $12 million and has set a deadline to return most of the funds.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 23, 2026 · 12:37 UTC2 min read

Chainflip says an attacker took about $12 million in cryptocurrency from its vaults, expanding the known loss from the Ethereum vault to include 122.86 bitcoin. The protocol is offering the attacker 10% of the funds if most of the assets are returned by Monday, November 23.

In a November 22 report, The Block put the total loss at about $12 million, after reporting an earlier $4.86 million drain of ether and stablecoins. Chainflip Labs’ message to the attacker lists the assets taken on November 21: 122.86137042 BTC, 520.2243399 ETH, 3,249,631.41 USDC and 758,141.33 USDT.

The bitcoin was taken from Chainflip’s Bitcoin vault, alongside assets drained from its Ethereum vault. The Block reported that the bitcoin was worth about $7.1 million at the time. The combined dollar figure is an estimate; the listed token amounts are the figures Chainflip gave.

Chainflip says it traced the attack’s preparation

Chainflip’s message says the attacker used Tornado Cash, a service that mixes cryptocurrency transactions, for funding on November 11. It also says a validator account was registered on November 20 and that Chainflip traced the server behind it.

The company says it identified how the key was exposed and has prevented the same kind of attack from happening again. Those are Chainflip’s statements; the message does not provide a technical explanation of the vulnerability.

The Block reported that the ether and stablecoin proceeds had been sent to Tornado Cash, while the stolen bitcoin had not moved from the address where it arrived. It also reported Chainflip’s claim that it had identified and fixed the exploited vulnerability.

Return offer runs until November 23

Chainflip says the attacker can keep 10% as a bounty if 110.57523337 BTC and 2,618.1 ETH reach the return addresses by 18:38 UTC on November 23. The company says it will treat the return as a “white hat” recovery—a term for funds returned by someone who reports a security flaw—and withdraw its reports and complaints to law enforcement.

Chainflip says it has flagged the attacker’s addresses with blockchain analytics firms and is monitoring withdrawals from the Tornado Cash pools used. The Block reported that the offer was also sent on-chain, meaning it was recorded on a public blockchain.

The deadline is the next stated step in the recovery effort. Neither the offer nor Chainflip’s account confirms that any funds have been returned.
