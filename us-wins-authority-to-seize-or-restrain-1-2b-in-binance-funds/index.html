US wins authority to seize or restrain $1.2B in Binance funds

The Justice Department won court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion tied to the Binance hack, part of $2.7 billion frozen so far.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 19, 2026 · 23:25 UTC2 min read

US authorities have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a step toward recovering funds from a hack that drained more than $6 billion from the exchange. The Justice Department’s announcement says the court-authorized amount is part of a larger pool of frozen assets.

More than $2.7 billion has been frozen

The theft took place on or about Oct. 29. The Justice Department says members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance.

Since then, US agencies and private-sector partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion in stolen funds. Freezing assets can stop them from moving while authorities work to recover them. The latest court order covers more than $1.2 billion of that total and permits the government to seize or restrain the funds.

The distinction matters: the order does not mean all $1.2 billion has been returned to Binance or its users. The Associated Press report says the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered assets.

Tracing and recovery are still underway

The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are ongoing. It says TraderTraitor actors continue to move the funds through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between blockchain networks; mixers obscure transaction trails.

Last week, the FBI urged virtual-asset service providers to block transactions involving addresses the group is using to launder the stolen Binance funds. The agencies are working with the US Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation to recover the assets.

The seizure announcement also covered a separate North Korean-linked scheme called FaceStealer. The Justice Department says that campaign stole more than $400 million in cryptocurrency over the last 18 months. It is separate from the Binance theft, and the $400 million is not part of the recovery figures tied to Binance.

For Binance users, the key figure is the more than $2.7 billion frozen so far, rather than the $1.2 billion covered by the court authorization alone. Authorities have not said how much of the frozen crypto will ultimately be forfeited or returned, and the recovery process remains active.
