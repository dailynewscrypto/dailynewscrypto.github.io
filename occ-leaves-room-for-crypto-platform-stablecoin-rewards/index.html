OCC leaves room for crypto platform stablecoin rewards

The OCC finalized its GENIUS Act rule without a blanket ban on platform rewards, leaving some stablecoin programs for case-by-case review from January 2027.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomOct 27, 2026 · 14:13 UTC2 min read

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency finalized a stablecoin rule that keeps the ban on issuers paying interest to coin holders but leaves some rewards offered by crypto platforms for case-by-case review. The distinction matters to customers because platforms may still offer rewards, though the rule does not approve every program.

The OCC’s October 21 announcement says its final rule implements the GENIUS Act, the federal law setting rules for payment stablecoins. The rule takes effect January 18, 2027. The OCC oversees national banks and some other stablecoin issuers.

Can stablecoin issuers pay interest directly?

No. The law bars permitted payment stablecoin issuers from paying interest or yield to holders solely for holding, using or keeping the coin. Stablecoins are crypto assets designed to hold a steady value, often pegged to the U.S. dollar.

The OCC’s final rule in the Federal Register says the agency considered a range of public comments on how broadly to apply that ban. Some commenters wanted it to cover indirect payments and rewards tied to transactions. Others argued for a narrower reading.

What does the rule mean for exchange rewards?

The OCC removed a proposed presumption that certain payments routed through an issuer’s affiliate would count as prohibited issuer payments. Instead, it says it will apply the law to the facts and circumstances of each case. That leaves room for some exchange or platform rewards, but it does not give them automatic clearance.

The agency also declined to define “interest” or “yield” in the rule. It said it would interpret those terms based on the facts before it. So a reward’s label alone does not settle whether it breaks the law.

Why are banks concerned about the change?

Banks argue that crypto rewards could draw customer money away from bank deposits, which banks use to support lending. The American Bankers Association said the OCC’s decision could threaten funds banks use to make loans, according to the Associated Press.

The debate turns on who pays the reward and why. The issuer ban remains in place, while a platform’s program will depend on its structure and circumstances. Customers should not assume a stablecoin reward is the same as interest in a bank account: stablecoins do not have the same federal deposit insurance as bank deposits.
