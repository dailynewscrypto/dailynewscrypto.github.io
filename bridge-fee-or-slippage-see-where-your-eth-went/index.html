Bridge fee or slippage? See where your ETH went

Received less ETH than sent after bridging? For ETH-to-ETH transfers, fees usually explain the gap: source gas and any bridge fee, not slippage.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 27, 2026 · 22:31 UTC3 min read

After a Base bridge transfer, receiving less ETH than you sent usually means fees, not slippage: an ETH-to-ETH route has no token swap, so there is no price to slip.

How to account for the ETH

An ETH bridge transfer can involve a source gas fee and a bridge fee, and they come out in different ways. Gas is paid on Ethereum to process the sending transaction. A bridge fee may be deducted from the ETH delivered on the other side.

Take a transfer where you send 0.5 ETH through Across Protocol. Your wallet balance drops by 0.5012 ETH, including 0.0012 ETH in source gas. You receive 0.4995 ETH on Base. Subtract the gas from the wallet drop first: 0.5012 minus 0.0012 leaves 0.5 ETH sent. The 0.0005 ETH difference between that sent amount and the 0.4995 ETH received is the bridge fee.

Across deducts its relayer and liquidity provider (LP) fee from the amount received, and its quote shows that fee. Compare the quoted output with the destination amount. The source transaction’s gas is separate, so don’t count it again as a deduction from the quoted output.

The native Base bridge credits an ETH deposit 1:1, so the amount deposited is the amount credited. Gas is paid separately on Ethereum from the sending wallet. The Base bridge connects Ethereum and Base for moving supported assets.

To check the records, view the sending transaction on Etherscan and the destination transaction on Basescan. The source record shows the gas fee, while the destination record shows the ETH credited. For the native Base bridge, that credited amount should match the deposit; the wallet’s extra drop is gas.

When slippage can explain a gap

Slippage can explain a lower output only when the route includes a token swap. An ETH-to-ETH transfer has no swap step, so its output does not change with a token price during the transfer.

Say the received amount is lower than the bridge quote. Open the route details and look for a swap that converts ETH to another token or converts another token back to ETH. That step can make the output differ from the quote. Without a swap, compare the quoted output with the amount credited and account for any stated bridge fee.

For a completed ETH-to-ETH transfer, subtract source gas from the sending wallet’s balance drop, then compare the remaining sent amount with the ETH credited. That shows whether the difference is the quoted bridge fee; investigate the route details if it is larger than the quote allows.
