OCC urges community banks to explore stablecoin issuance

The OCC is urging community banks to consider issuing dollar-linked stablecoins through subsidiaries, giving smaller lenders a path into payments under new federal rules.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 3, 2026 · 07:59 UTC3 min read

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency is urging community banks to explore issuing payment stablecoins, opening a possible route for smaller lenders into digital payments. In remarks published Oct. 27, Comptroller Jonathan V. Gould said the agency’s final rule lets interested banks issue them through a subsidiary. The OCC’s remarks on community banks say the requirements are meant to fit an issuer’s size and risk. A stablecoin is a crypto token designed to hold a steady value, often tied to the U.S. dollar.

The OCC’s call comes as federal rules for payment stablecoins take shape under the GENIUS Act. CoinDesk reported that the regulator encouraged community banks to seek permission to issue their own dollar-pegged tokens, directly or through subsidiaries.

How could a community bank issue a stablecoin?

A bank could set up a subsidiary to issue a payment stablecoin, subject to OCC approval and supervision. The token would be designed for payments and tied to a fixed value, such as one U.S. dollar. The issuer is the entity responsible for redeeming or repurchasing the token for that fixed value.

The OCC says the final rule applies the same standards to bank and nonbank issuers, while tailoring requirements to the size and risk of the issuer. Gould said this approach is intended to let smaller banks explore the business without a compliance burden designed only for the largest institutions.

When do the new rules take effect?

The OCC’s final rule is set to take effect Jan. 18, 2027. The agency said it will announce separately when it begins accepting applications and waiver requests. CoinDesk reported that the application process had not yet opened, pending approval of federal paperwork.

Existing issuers that submit substantially complete applications by the effective date may request temporary waivers to keep operating while regulators review them. The OCC said it generally expects to grant waivers where appropriate.

What could this mean for local banks?

The OCC says community banks have a role in the future of payments. If banks choose to issue stablecoins, they could offer customers another way to make digital payments. The agency’s announcement does not say that any community bank has applied or committed to launch one.

The plan also arrives amid disagreement over how stablecoins could affect traditional banking. CoinDesk reported that banking groups have warned that yield-bearing tokens could pull deposits away from banks. The OCC’s invitation gives smaller lenders a chance to participate in the market as those rules and concerns develop.
