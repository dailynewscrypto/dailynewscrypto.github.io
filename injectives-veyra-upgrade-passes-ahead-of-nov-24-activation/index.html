Injective’s Veyra upgrade passes ahead of Nov. 24 activation

Injective’s Veyra upgrade passed governance, clearing a path to a Nov. 24 mainnet halt and new cross-chain and institutional trading tools for builders.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 19, 2026 · 22:07 UTC2 min read

Injective voters approved the Veyra mainnet upgrade, clearing the way for a planned Nov. 24 network halt and a new set of cross-chain and institutional trading tools. The changes are part of software version v1.21.0.

According to the Plebis governance record for proposal 717, voting closed on Nov. 16 with 42.69% participation, above the 33.4% quorum needed for a valid vote. The proposal passed with 16,236,896 INJ voting yes, 80,589 voting no and one voting no with veto. Another 7,745,866 INJ abstained.

Veyra adds cross-chain transfers and trading tools

The approved proposal adds Inter-Blockchain Communication (IBC), a system for moving assets between blockchains, to connect Injective’s Ethereum-compatible environment with other networks. That could give applications built on Injective a way to connect tokenized assets with liquidity across chains.

Veyra also adds request-for-quote infrastructure, which lets traders ask for prices, and expands atomic swaps, trades that either complete fully or do not take effect. The Defiant’s report on the passed vote and upgrade scope says the package focuses on trading and connectivity tools. Injective’s planned CypherOS privacy platform remains on its roadmap; the upgrade does not launch that platform.

Activation is tied to a block height

The proposal sets the upgrade for block height 193,145,000, with Nov. 24 as the target date. Injective’s chain is expected to halt at that height and resume using the v1.21.0 software. The date is a target tied to when the chain reaches the block, rather than a fixed activation time.

Validators and other node operators will need to move to the new software for the chain to resume after the halt. For users, the vote settles whether Injective will adopt the upgrade; the next milestone is the planned network change. The governance record describes the planned additions as infrastructure for cross-chain asset movement and institutional trading workflows. It does not say that Veyra itself will issue new real-world assets.
