Mint Authority and Who Can Expand a Token’s Supply

What is mint authority on a token? It lets one address create supply; judge the holder’s control, program rules and upgrade authority before you buy.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 19, 2026 · 16:13 UTC3 min read

Mint authority controls who can create supply

What does it mean if a token still has mint authority? It means one address can authorize the creation of new tokens, so that address can expand the supply. On a Solana Program Library or Token-2022 mint, only the mint authority can sign a MintTo instruction, and there is no built-in cap on how much it can issue. A rugcheck adds useful context by reviewing the token for warning signs that could point to a scam or rug pull.

The key question is who controls that address. A wallet authority acts at its owner’s discretion. A program-derived address, or PDA, follows the program’s code and upgrade authority instead of a person’s private key. That can limit when the program can mint, but it does not make the authority safe by itself: the program’s rules and who can change them matter.

Take two otherwise identical tokens, each with the same active mint authority and planned supply. In the first case, the creator wallet holds the authority. The creator can decide to issue a large new batch and sell it into the pool. In the second, a PDA holds it and the program’s rules permit minting only for scheduled rewards. The difference is control: the wallet owner chooses when to mint, while the program can mint only under its code as it stands. If someone can upgrade that program, they may be able to change those rules.

Projects may keep mint authority to create emissions, distribute staking rewards or support a bridge. Those uses can explain why authority remains active, but they do not remove the risk of added supply. Mint authority creates tokens; freeze authority has a different role and can block individual accounts from transferring them.

How to judge the authority and related risks

Open the authority address on Solscan to see whether it is a wallet or a program. For a wallet, consider whether one person can issue tokens at their discretion. For a program, look at the rules that govern minting and the program’s upgrade authority. After any authority change, run rugcheck again to review the token’s warning signs with the updated setup in mind.

How do you revoke mint authority?

Set the mint authority to none with SetAuthority. That change is irreversible, so the token can no longer mint new supply through that authority. Confirm that the project does not need future emissions, rewards or bridge issuance before treating revocation as the right choice.

Is freeze authority worse than mint authority?

Neither is automatically worse; they give different powers. Mint authority can increase supply, while freeze authority can stop individual accounts from transferring. Judge each by who controls it and how that power could affect holders.

An active mint authority matters because its holder can authorize new supply. A person’s wallet gives that person discretion; a program-derived address follows code that may itself be upgradeable. Check who controls the address and its rules, then use a rugcheck as one part of your decision before committing.
