Chainflip halts swaps after $4.86M Ethereum vault drain

Chainflip halted swaps after $4.86 million left its Ethereum vault, and FLIP fell more than 30%; the team is investigating and has offered a return bounty.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 22, 2026 · 18:17 UTC2 min read

Chainflip halted deposits, withdrawals and swaps on Nov. 21 after about $4.86 million in assets left its Ethereum vault in one transaction. FLIP fell more than 30%, according to CoinDesk’s report. Chainflip said it was investigating a potential exploit and put the protocol into “full safe mode.”

The transaction took 520.22 ether, about 3.25 million USDC and 758,000 USDT, according to blockchain trackers cited by CoinDesk. It carried a valid signature from the shared key validators use to approve vault payouts. That has raised the possibility that the signing key was compromised, but Chainflip had not confirmed the cause.

Chainflip uses validators to authorize withdrawals from its vaults. The Block reported that 100 of its 150 validators are required to sign transactions. A valid signature let the vault release the funds, while the safe mode stopped further activity. CoinDesk reported that the stolen stablecoins were converted to ether and nearly all the funds went into Tornado Cash, a transaction mixer, within nine minutes.

The Ethereum loss was not the only reported theft. BeInCrypto reported that Chainflip’s own tally showed more than 122 Bitcoin taken from its Bitcoin vault as well. The protocol offered the attacker a 10% bounty to return 90% of the funds by 18:38 UTC on Nov. 23. Chainflip said it would treat a return of both the Bitcoin and Ethereum-side funds as a white-hat recovery and drop its police reports.

The incident came about ten weeks after a separate loss from Chainflip’s Tron vault. In September, an attacker exploited how the protocol handled transaction memos, leading to six withdrawals being paid twice and a loss of about 736,000 USDT, CoinDesk reported. Chainflip’s validators later approved reimbursements for those affected users. At the time of the latest reports, the protocol was still investigating the November incident, and no cause or reimbursement plan for the new losses had been confirmed.
