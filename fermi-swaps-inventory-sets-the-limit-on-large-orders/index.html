Fermi Swap’s Inventory Sets the Limit on Large Orders

Fermi Swap fills trades from its own token stock, so large orders depend on available inventory, the quote and what remains when a transaction executes on chain.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomOct 30, 2026 · 02:22 UTC3 min read

Fermi Swap’s practical limit on a large token order is the amount of the requested token available in its inventory when the trade executes. DefiLlama describes FermiSwap as a proprietary automated market maker (a system that quotes and fills swaps) on Ethereum, using its own token inventory rather than shared liquidity pools. That makes available stock part of the trade’s price and whether it can be filled.

For readers assessing a large swap, fermi swap is best understood through that inventory model. DefiLlama says its tracker counts only trades FermiSwap fills from its inventory. Its methodology lists WETH, USDT, USDC, cbBTC and WBTC in the inventory vault, but it does not give a per-token order cap or a live amount available for each trade.

Why does inventory limit a large Fermi Swap order?

A swap needs enough of the output token—the asset the trader wants to receive—to cover the requested amount. If an order asks for more than the market maker can supply, it may not fill in full. Even below that point, a larger order can receive a worse quote as the available stock and pricing terms change with size.

This differs from a pool where many liquidity providers contribute funds. With an inventory-based market maker, the relevant supply is the stock it holds for that asset. A quote is therefore tied to the pair and amount requested; it is not a promise that any size can trade at the same rate.

How can a trader assess the size before swapping?

Check the quote for the exact amount you plan to trade. Then look at the minimum amount received, if the interface offers that setting. It defines the lowest output you will accept; if the trade cannot meet it, execution should fail instead of completing below that threshold.

  • Compare quotes for the full order and smaller portions. A sharp change in output can show that size affects the terms.
  • Check which token you receive and how much the quote says will arrive.
  • Allow for fees and network costs when judging the effective price.
  • Review the transaction details before signing, since a quote can change before the transaction is included on chain.

Splitting a large order can reduce the amount demanded in each trade, but it does not guarantee a better total price. Later quotes may move, and each transaction can add network costs. The useful comparison is the expected total output after fees, not just the first smaller quote.

What does Fermi Swap’s inventory model mean in practice?

It means there is no verified universal order ceiling to quote from the public inventory data alone. The available stock can differ by token and change as trades are filled or inventory is replenished. A large order may be possible when the requested asset is well stocked, while a smaller one may face tighter terms when stock is low.

For most readers, the better choice is to size the trade from the live quote and set an acceptable minimum output. Treat the quoted amount as specific to that order and moment. Fermi Swap’s inventory model makes order size a key input, so compare the full trade terms before deciding whether to proceed.
