What Is Blockchain Finality, and Why Do Bridges Wait?

Transaction finality in blockchain marks when a transfer is settled: Bitcoin gains confidence with blocks, while Ethereum finalizes checkpoints in about 12.8 minutes.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 24, 2026 · 02:49 UTC3 min read

Transaction finality in blockchain is the point at which a confirmed transfer is treated as settled and no longer expected to be reversed. Recent blocks can be reorganized, so a transaction’s inclusion in a block is not always the same as final settlement.

How do Bitcoin and Ethereum reach finality?

Bitcoin has probabilistic finality: confidence in a transaction grows as more blocks are added on top of the one that contains it. The Bitcoin whitepaper’s section 11 explains that the odds of a successful reversal fall with each added block. There is no single block count that makes every Bitcoin transfer final; the receiver sets how many confirmations it will accept.

Ethereum uses economic finality: Casper FFG marks blocks finalized at checkpoints, with reversal carrying a severe economic cost for validators. Ethereum slots last 12 seconds, and each epoch contains 32 slots. Finality takes about two epochs: 2 × 32 × 12 seconds, or roughly 12.8 minutes. Until then, a block may be included without being finalized.

That difference matters when a transfer crosses chains. RenBridge used RenVM to mint renBTC on another chain after a Bitcoin deposit reached a set number of confirmations. For someone waiting on that transfer, RenBridge shows why the confirmation threshold affects when the receiving side can issue tokens.

Say you deposit Bitcoin to be represented as renBTC. If RenVM minted tokens as soon as the deposit appeared in a block, a later reorganization could remove that deposit. The renBTC would already be issued, but its Bitcoin backing could be gone. Waiting for more confirmations lowers that risk; it does not make the risk mathematically zero.

Confirmation and finality are related, but they describe different things. A confirmation means a block includes the transaction; each block added afterward increases the confirmation count. Finality means the network has reached a point where reversal is no longer expected, either because the odds have fallen with confirmations or because a finalized checkpoint makes reversal economically costly. Mixing them up can make an included transfer seem settled before the receiver is ready to rely on it.

So, finality tells you when a transfer can be relied on as settled, while confirmations or a finalized checkpoint provide the basis for that judgment. Because recent blocks can change, bridges wait for enough evidence before they issue tokens against a deposit.

What else should you know about finality?

How many confirmations do exchanges require for Bitcoin deposits?

There is no single Bitcoin confirmation count for every exchange. Each exchange sets its own requirement, and the wait depends on that policy and how quickly new blocks arrive. Check the exchange’s deposit status or policy for the count that applies to your transfer.

Can a finalized Ethereum block ever be reversed?

A finalized Ethereum block is designed to be extremely difficult to reverse, not physically impossible to change. Reversing it would require a major failure and severe economic penalties for validators, which is why Ethereum treats finalized checkpoints as settled.
