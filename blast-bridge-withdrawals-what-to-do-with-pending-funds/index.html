Blast Bridge withdrawals: what to do with pending funds

Blast withdrawals now take about a day, but the normal interface is available only until October 26; pending requests still need proving and claiming on Ethereum.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomOct 21, 2026 · 23:20 UTC3 min read

Blast bridge withdrawals move assets from Blast to Ethereum, and users with pending requests may still need to prove or claim them before the funds arrive. The network is winding down, and its normal interface is scheduled to remain available through October 26, 2026. The current bridge page says withdrawals take about one day, though some tokens need extra steps.

The route depends on where a withdrawal is in the process and which asset is moving. Blast’s published timetable is also noted at blastbridge.org. A pending request is not the same as a completed transfer: check its status in the bridge’s History tab, using the same wallet that started it.

How do I finish a pending Blast bridge withdrawal?

Open the bridge’s History tab and continue any request that has not been claimed. A standard ETH withdrawal starts on Blast, then requires a proof transaction on Ethereum. After the challenge period—a wait that lets the network check the withdrawal—you return to History and claim the ETH.

The bridge instructions say the proof step can take up to an hour to become available, followed by a one-day challenge period. Keep some ETH in the same wallet on Ethereum to pay transaction fees for the remaining steps, and leave a little ETH on Blast for the first transaction. The wallet address receiving the funds on Ethereum is the same one used to withdraw from Blast.

USDB and BLAST have extra steps. USDB arrives on Ethereum as DAI, and may take up to another day to process after finalization. BLAST may ask for an approval transaction before the withdrawal. WETH is not supported directly by the bridge and must first be unwrapped to ETH on Blast.

What changes after October 26?

Blast says users can use its normal interface through October 26, 2026. After that, assets are still meant to be withdrawable, but users will need to interact directly with Blast’s bridge contracts on Ethereum rather than use the usual interface. The team has said it will publish detailed instructions for that route before the deadline.

That makes an unfinished request harder to manage after the date: the normal History screen may no longer be available to resume it. If a withdrawal is already pending, finish its available steps through the interface while it remains open. Do not assume that a submitted request has reached Ethereum until the final claim is complete and the receiving wallet shows the asset.

Which assets arrive on Ethereum, and in what form?

ETH arrives as ETH, while Blast’s withdrawal guide says USDB arrives as DAI. BLAST arrives as BLAST. WETH must be unwrapped to ETH before bridging. These differences affect both the steps and the funds available after the transfer.

  • ETH: prove, wait through the challenge period, then claim.
  • USDB: expect DAI on Ethereum and an extra processing step.
  • BLAST: allow for approval and withdrawal transactions.
  • WETH: unwrap to ETH on Blast first.

For most holders, completing the withdrawal through the normal interface before October 26 is the clearest route: it provides a visible transaction history and avoids relying on contract instructions that have not yet been published. Keep enough ETH on both networks for fees, and treat each request as unfinished until the final asset lands on Ethereum.
