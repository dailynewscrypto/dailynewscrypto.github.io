Why a USDT Bridge Transfer Gets Stuck on Finality

Why is my USDT bridge transfer stuck? It is usually waiting for source finality or a relayer; check the bridge explorer to see whether to wait or claim.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 27, 2026 · 03:27 UTC3 min read

A USDT bridge transfer that has left your wallet is usually still pending, waiting for source-chain finality or a relayer. A USDT bridge helps move USDT to another network; to trace a stuck transfer, search its source transaction hash in the explorer for the bridge you used. The source-chain explorer shows only that chain’s side, so it may not show whether the destination delivery or a claim is complete.

How to tell if a USDT bridge transfer is stuck

Start with the source transaction hash and your wallet address. Open the hash on the source chain explorer, such as Etherscan or Tronscan, and confirm the transaction succeeded. Then paste that same hash into the bridge’s explorer, such as LayerZero Scan or Wormholescan. Its message status can show whether the transfer is waiting, ready to claim, or completed.

The most common cause is that the source chain has not reached finality, the point at which a transaction is treated as settled. The bridge may still be waiting to verify the message or for a relayer, which passes it to the destination. If the bridge explorer shows a pending or in-progress message, wait and check that status again; sending another transfer could create a second deposit.

Some transfers arrive on the destination but need a manual claim. The bridge explorer may show that the message is ready to redeem even though your wallet has not received USDT. Wormhole transfers can require manual redemption, which uses destination-chain gas. Follow the claim prompt in the bridge flow, then look up the destination transaction to confirm receipt. Wormhole’s documentation describes this redemption step.

A failed or still-pending source transaction cannot deliver a deposit. If the source explorer marks it failed, there is no successful deposit for the bridge to complete. If it remains pending, low gas may be holding it up. Resolve the transaction on the source chain first, then start a new transfer only after confirming the original outcome.

Some waits are expected. Arbitrum’s canonical withdrawals to Ethereum have a challenge period of about seven days. The Arbitrum documentation explains this wait; it is different from a transfer that is ready for a destination claim.

What to do next for a USDT cross-chain transfer

Copy the source transaction hash. Confirm success on the source explorer, check the hash in the bridge explorer, and claim on the destination if prompted. Your task is done when the destination explorer shows the USDT received.

If the status does not fit these cases, keep the source hash and bridge explorer status, then contact only the bridge’s official support. LayerZero Scan supports message-status lookups, but the bridge’s own status and official support are the useful next places to investigate.

Related

How long does a USDT bridge transfer take?

It depends on source finality, message verification, relaying, and any claim step. An Arbitrum canonical withdrawal to Ethereum can wait about seven days for its challenge period.

Can I cancel a USDT bridge transfer?

First check the source transaction and bridge status. A failed source transaction did not deposit funds; a pending message may still be delivered, while a ready-to-claim message may need redemption. Use the status to choose the next step and avoid sending a duplicate.
