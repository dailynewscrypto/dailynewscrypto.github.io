X Routes Cashtag Trades to Coinbase, Kraken and Other Brokers

X has launched U.S. cashtag links to trading partners, letting users move from crypto and stock conversations to charts and orders on external platforms.

The Web3 News EditorsSep 24, 20262 min read

X has launched cashtag links that let U.S. users move from conversations about crypto and stocks to trading partners, connecting market discussion on the social network to platforms such as Coinbase and Kraken. CoinDesk’s report on the launch says users can tap symbols such as $BTC or $TSLA to see charts and related posts, then use a “Trade” button to continue with a partner.

How does a cashtag become a trade?

The cashtag opens an asset page inside X, where the user can inspect its live chart and market conversation. Tapping “Trade” sends the user to a participating platform, where they sign in or create an account and complete the transaction. It works like a signpost from a market conversation to a brokerage: X provides the route, while the partner handles the trade.

Interactive Brokers, one of the partners, described the same flow in its September 15 integration announcement: users can search tickers including $AAPL and $BTC, then select IBKR from the ticker page to research or place a trade. The feature is currently available to U.S.-based investors, according to the firm.

Which platforms are involved?

CoinDesk lists Interactive Brokers, Moomoo, Gemini, Kraken and Coinbase as X’s partners. That gives users several destinations from the cashtag page, but the trade itself takes place on the selected partner platform, not inside X. The integration therefore shortens the path from seeing a ticker in a post to reaching a trading service; it does not make X the venue executing the order.

What changes for crypto users?

For crypto users, the new step is a direct handoff from a ticker page such as $BTC to a partner exchange. The feature also covers stock and ETF tickers, bringing those asset discussions into the same cashtag interface. Interactive Brokers says its integration supports searches for stock or crypto tickers, while CoinDesk reports that users complete transactions through the listed partner platforms.

The practical change is in the sequence: users encounter a ticker in a post, open its page, review the chart and conversation, then leave X for a partner to trade. X links discovery and trading access in one flow, while account access and order completion remain with the partner.
