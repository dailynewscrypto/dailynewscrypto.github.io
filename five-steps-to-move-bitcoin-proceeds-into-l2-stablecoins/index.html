Five steps to move bitcoin proceeds into L2 stablecoins

Moving bitcoin proceeds into an L2 stablecoin takes a route, a destination and enough gas; check each before you send funds or approve a swap.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomOct 16, 2026 · 18:12 UTC3 min read

Moving bitcoin proceeds into a stablecoin on a layer 2 network takes five checks: choose the token and network, prepare the destination wallet, select a route, review the quote and confirm the funds arrived. A layer 2, or L2, is a network built to handle transactions alongside a main blockchain, often with lower fees. The route matters because a Bitcoin transaction cannot directly place USDC on an L2.

Which stablecoin and L2 should you choose?

Step one is to choose the exact stablecoin and network you want to use. USDC on Base and USDC on Arbitrum are separate assets on separate networks; a wallet showing one does not mean it can use the other. Check that the app or service you plan to use supports the exact pair, such as BTC to USDC on Base. The thor swap guide covers cross-chain execution in more detail, including the route choices this article only touches on.

Step two is to set up a wallet that can receive that token on that L2. Copy its address from the wallet and confirm the selected network. If your bitcoin is held on an exchange, check whether it can withdraw BTC to a wallet you control; otherwise, you may need to sell it there and withdraw a supported stablecoin directly to the L2.

How do you choose and check the route?

Step three is to choose a route that accepts BTC and sends the selected stablecoin to your chosen L2. Some routes may swap through another asset or send funds to an intermediate network before a bridge moves them to the L2. A bridge is a service that transfers assets between networks. Check the route shown on screen rather than assuming the swap is direct.

Step four is to review the quote before signing. Compare the amount of stablecoin you will receive with the BTC amount you are sending, and check the fees, expected time and minimum output. Fees and prices can change while a quote is open. The key checks are:

  • The source is Bitcoin and the destination is the intended L2.
  • The token name and network match your wallet.
  • The quoted fees and minimum output are acceptable.
  • The destination address is copied from your own wallet.

Send a small test amount first if the route or wallet is unfamiliar. This adds a transaction fee, but it can catch a wrong address or network before you move a larger balance.

How do you confirm the stablecoins arrived?

Step five is to wait for the route to finish, then check the transaction status and your wallet balance on the destination network. If the balance is not visible, confirm that the wallet is displaying the right L2 and token; adding a token display does not move funds. Keep some of the received stablecoin or the network’s gas token for future transactions. Gas is the fee paid to process actions on a blockchain.

For most readers, the better route is the one that clearly names the destination network, shows the full expected output and sends to a wallet they control. A route with fewer screens is not automatically cheaper or safer. Verify the token, network and final amount before approving it.
