Can smart contract wallets use cross chain bridges safely?

Smart contract wallets can send through most cross chain bridges, but the destination address must be controlled by a wallet deployed on the destination…

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 28, 2026 · 06:10 UTC4 min read

Smart contract wallets can send through most cross chain bridges, but the destination address must be controlled by a wallet deployed on the destination chain. A Safe or ERC-4337 account is a contract on each chain where it has been deployed, so the same address on another chain may not be a usable wallet.

How does a smart contract wallet move funds across a bridge?

You start by choosing the source chain, the asset and amount, and the destination chain. The bridge or its route provider takes the source tokens, carries out the transfer across chains, and delivers the destination asset to the address you specify. The key decision is that recipient address: choose one you can control on the destination chain.

A Safe deployment is chain-specific. Its address may be calculated with CREATE2, but matching the address on another chain depends on using the same factory, singleton and setup. If those conditions differ, the address can differ too. Even when the address matches, the Safe contract must be deployed there before its owners can use it.

This matters because an undeployed Safe address is just an address on that chain; there is no Safe contract there to enforce its owners’ approvals. Until deployment, nobody can sign transactions as that Safe. Someone else may be able to deploy a Safe at that address first, creating a risk that the intended owners cannot recover the funds.

Signature checks also vary by route. EIP-1271 is the standard that lets a contract wallet validate a signature. A bridge flow that supports EIP-1271 can accept a Safe’s contract signature; a step that insists on an externally owned account (EOA) signature may fail for a contract wallet. Read the route’s signature and transaction requirements, especially if it asks you to sign a message as well as approve tokens.

When a route starts from a Safe, confirm who controls the destination before signing. In a lifi bridge route, LI.FI is a cross-chain bridge aggregator that finds available transfer routes; you can set a custom destination address instead of defaulting to the Safe’s address. For a short explanation of whether a lifi bridge needs a wallet, see the related answer. The recipient can be an EOA you control or a Safe already deployed on the destination chain.

Safe owners also need to account for quote expiry. A bridge quote can expire while signers are gathering the required threshold, and the final transaction may then fail or no longer match the quoted route. Collect approvals against the current proposal, execute promptly once the threshold is met, and re-quote before the last signature if the quote has expired or its terms have changed.

The June 2022 Wintermute 20M OP incident shows why deployment status matters. The public incident record describes OP sent to a Wintermute Safe address on Optimism where the Safe had not been deployed; an attacker deployed there first and took control of the funds. The transfer reached the address, but the intended Safe owners did not yet have a deployed contract through which to exercise control.

For routine transfers, use a destination EOA when you need a simple recipient, or verify that your Safe is deployed on the destination chain before using its address. For treasury transfers, confirm the network, recipient and token with the signers, then allow time for the multisig threshold without assuming the quote will remain live.

Related

Can a Safe multisig bridge through WalletConnect?

Yes, when the wallet interface, bridge route and connected application support Safe transactions. WalletConnect can connect the interface, while the Safe still requires its owners’ threshold approvals before execution. Review the transaction details in the Safe interface, including the chain, token allowance, recipient and expiry, and make sure the final proposal is submitted before the route quote expires.

Does account abstraction remove the need for destination gas?

No. ERC-4337 lets an account bundle actions such as approving a token and initiating a bridge into one user operation, so you may not need to submit those source-chain steps separately. That batching does not automatically pay for a later transaction on the destination chain. Receiving tokens usually needs no destination transaction, but using them in a swap or another contract call does; check whether the route includes destination gas or whether you must fund it yourself.
