How to reach a lending market on another chain

A bridge can move tokens toward a lending market, but the destination still needs the right asset, liquidity and a separate supply transaction.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomOct 16, 2026 · 21:13 UTC3 min read

To use a lending market on another chain, you usually need to move a supported token to that network, then supply it in a separate transaction. A bridge moves assets between chains; it does not automatically deposit them into a lending market. The distinction matters because the destination market may accept only certain tokens, and the transfer may arrive as a wrapped version of the asset.

How does a cross-chain transfer reach a lending market?

A cross-chain route links a token on one network to an asset on another. Depending on the route, the service may lock tokens on the source chain and release or mint a corresponding token on the destination, or swap into another asset along the way. For more detail on route choices and transfer steps, see this Biswap guide to routes for moving crypto across chains. The token you receive still has to match what the lending market lists.

Markets are deployed on specific chains, and each deployment has its own pools and rules. A token supplied on one chain does not automatically count as a deposit on another. For example, a lending protocol may offer a market on both Ethereum and Base, but balances and available assets are handled separately on each network.

What should you check before choosing a route?

Start from the lending market and work backwards. Confirm the destination chain and the exact token it accepts, then check that the route delivers that asset in a compatible form. A familiar ticker or token name is not enough: different contracts can represent different assets, even when their names look alike.

  • Supported asset: Check the token contract or the market’s own asset listing, especially for stablecoins and wrapped tokens.
  • Available liquidity: Make sure the market can accept more deposits. A supply cap is a limit on how much of an asset the market will take.
  • Route costs: Compare the swap price, bridge fee and network fees. A route that looks cheaper at the start may deliver less after all costs.
  • Destination gas: Keep enough of the destination chain’s fee token to approve and supply the asset after it arrives.

These checks can rule out a route before you pay to use it. If the destination asset differs from what the market accepts, you may need another swap, adding cost and another transaction.

How do you complete the supply?

First, send a small test amount if the route and token are unfamiliar. Wait for the transfer to complete, then confirm the asset and network in your wallet. Next, open the lending market on the destination chain, select the matching asset, review its supply terms and submit the supply transaction. The market may ask you to approve token use before the deposit; that approval is a separate permission transaction.

Supplying makes the asset available to borrowers and may earn interest, but the rate can change with borrowing demand and market settings. Deposited funds can also be subject to withdrawal limits when much of the pool is already borrowed. Reaching the right chain is only the first step: the better route is the one that delivers an accepted asset at a reasonable total cost, with enough liquidity left for the deposit and later withdrawal.
