Ethereum sets Dec. 15 test for Glamsterdam upgrade

Ethereum developers set Glamsterdam for Hoodi on Dec. 15, keeping a disputed storage-fee change that could force existing smart contracts to be fixed.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 28, 2026 · 04:21 UTC2 min read

Ethereum developers have set Dec. 15 for the Glamsterdam upgrade’s next public test, giving teams a deadline to address smart contracts that could fail under a disputed change to storage fees. The upgrade is scheduled to activate on the Hoodi testnet at 19:12 UTC, according to The Block’s report on the developers’ decision. Hoodi is a test network used to rehearse changes before they reach Ethereum’s main network.

What changed in the developers’ decision?

Core developers agreed to proceed with the current Glamsterdam specification, including EIP-8037, a proposal that raises the charge for adding permanent data to Ethereum. The Block reported the decision followed an all-core developers meeting. Developers did not set a mainnet activation date.

The Hoodi test was originally scheduled for Oct. 26, but was postponed after concerns that the new rules could affect existing programs. The Dec. 15 date is seven weeks later than first planned, CoinDesk reported in its account of the test schedule and contract risks.

Why could the storage-fee change affect contracts?

Smart contracts are programs that run on Ethereum. Under EIP-8037, creating a new contract would incur 183,600 units of gas in storage charges alone, before regular fees and a per-byte charge on its code, CoinDesk reported. The current flat base cost is 32,000 units. Gas measures the computing work a transaction uses.

Contracts built around the old prices could run out of gas partway through a transaction and fail. Ethereum Foundation developer Marius van der Wijden estimated that up to 15,000 of the network’s roughly 90 million contracts could be exposed, according to CoinDesk. The outlet reported that ChainSecurity and the Foundation had flagged about 200 trouble spots, including in bridges, wallets, exchanges, staking services and data-feeding oracles.

What needs to happen before the Hoodi test?

CoinDesk reported that most identified problems could be fixed by changing a setting, while 24 required upgrading programs or moving users and their funds. Teams had until Dec. 15 to address the most serious issues, with other fixes due by Jan. 10.

Glamsterdam also includes changes aimed at Ethereum’s scaling roadmap, such as block-level access lists, which record the state a block touches so client software can process transactions in parallel. The Hoodi test will let developers check the upgrade on a public network. The sources reported no confirmed mainnet date.
