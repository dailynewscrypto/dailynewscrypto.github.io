Liquidation Risk for a Beginner Borrower: How It Works

How to explain liquidation risk to a beginner borrower: collateral backs debt; below 1, health factor can trigger repayment and a bonus taken from collateral.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 21, 2026 · 18:17 UTC3 min read

To explain liquidation risk to a first-time borrower, say that collateral backs the loan and can be taken to repay debt if its value falls far enough. The protocol tracks that danger with a health factor; below 1, liquidators can repay part of the debt and receive collateral plus a bonus.

On SparkLend, the health factor reflects collateral value, the liquidation threshold and debt. A falling collateral price can lower it; accrued interest can also raise debt and lower it. The Aave V3 documentation describes the health factor formula and liquidation bonus, and SparkLend documentation says the protocol uses Aave V3 liquidation mechanics.

Borrowing again against supplied assets can add debt and leave less room for a price drop. That changes how much risk a friend takes in a recursive loan, as this explanation of how a 3x SparkLend loop changes risk shows.

What is liquidation, and what triggers it?

Liquidation is when collateral is taken to repay debt after a loan becomes too risky. It is allowed when the health factor falls below 1, which can happen when collateral loses value or interest accrues on the debt.

The health factor is calculated as collateral value multiplied by its liquidation threshold, divided by debt. The threshold is the share of collateral value used in that calculation. A lower health factor means less room for a price move or more interest to build before liquidation becomes possible.

When liquidation happens, a liquidator repays part of the debt and receives collateral worth more than the amount repaid. That extra amount is the liquidation bonus. The borrower loses the repaid slice of collateral plus the bonus, so liquidation can cost more than simply paying down the same amount of debt themselves.

What health factor should a first-time borrower aim for?

A first-time borrower should leave a buffer above 1, because a health factor just over 1 leaves little room for a price drop or growing debt. On SparkLend, a health factor of 1.5 means collateral could fall about 33% before liquidation, assuming debt and other prices stay unchanged.

The estimate comes from 1 − 1/HF: at 1.5, that is about 0.33. It is a rough measure of price room, not a promise that the loan is safe. A changing debt balance or other prices can alter the health factor while the borrower is watching it.

Does borrowing a stablecoin lower liquidation risk?

Borrowing a stablecoin does not remove liquidation risk: if collateral falls relative to the debt, the health factor can still drop below 1. Even if the stablecoin’s price stays near its peg, the borrower should watch collateral prices, accrued debt and the health factor.

For a first loan, explain the trigger and the cost together: below 1, a liquidator may repay part of the debt and take collateral at a bonus. Watching the health factor gives the borrower a clear signal of how much room remains.
