EVM Testnet Faucet: How Tokens Reach Your Wallet

An EVM testnet faucet sends test tokens to your wallet address, giving you funds to try apps and pay gas on that test network without using mainnet assets.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 23, 2026 · 23:13 UTC3 min read

An EVM testnet faucet sends test tokens to a wallet address so you can try transactions and apps without using mainnet funds.

What happens when you request testnet tokens?

For a testnet faucet, you provide your public wallet address and ask for tokens on a specific network. The faucet applies its own rules, then sends test tokens on that network to your address. Your wallet can use the resulting balance for gas or app testing there.

Say you want to try an EVM app on Sepolia, an Ethereum testnet. You copy your wallet’s public address and submit it to a Sepolia faucet. A faucet request should need that address only. Never share your seed phrase or private key; those protect control of your wallet, and a faucet does not need them to send tokens.

Faucet availability and request rules vary by network and provider. Take two otherwise identical cases: the same wallet requests Sepolia ETH from two providers. One provider accepts the request under its rules and sends tokens; the other may reject it if the request does not meet its eligibility rules. The network and wallet have not changed. The provider’s rules determine the different outcomes.

How do you use the tokens after they arrive?

Once the tokens arrive, select the matching testnet in your wallet and use its native coin to pay gas, the fee for a transaction on that network. On Sepolia, that means selecting Sepolia in MetaMask and using the received test ETH for Sepolia gas or app tests. The balance is usually enough to try an app or transaction, though the amount depends on the faucet.

Chainlist can help you find EVM network details, including testnets when “Include Testnets” is enabled. For background, see what Chainlist’s Include Testnets setting shows. Chainlist helps with network discovery; the faucet supplies tokens, while adding or selecting the network in your wallet lets it connect to that network.

Related

Where can I get Sepolia test ETH?

Use a Sepolia faucet that accepts your wallet address. Each provider may set its own eligibility rules.

Can I use testnet tokens on mainnet?

No. Testnet tokens belong to the test network and are not mainnet funds.

  • Request tokens with your public address from a faucet for the network you want to try.
  • Use that network’s native coin for its gas and app tests.
  • Keep your seed phrase and private key private; testnet tokens are for testing.
