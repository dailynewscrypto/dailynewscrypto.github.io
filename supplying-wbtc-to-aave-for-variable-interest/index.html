Supplying WBTC to Aave for variable interest

To supply wrapped bitcoin on Aave to earn interest, deposit WBTC into a market and receive aWBTC; the rate varies with borrowing demand and market capacity.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 25, 2026 · 07:17 UTC3 min read

To earn interest on wrapped Bitcoin through Aave, supply WBTC to a market and receive aWBTC, whose balance rises as interest accrues. The rate changes with market use, and the market’s supply cap can stop a deposit.

  • You need WBTC, a Web3 wallet and the network’s gas token.
  • Borrowers pay interest that flows to suppliers.
  • It may suit a long hold when gas costs are low.

Before choosing a wrapped asset, consider how it is backed and redeemed. RenBridge was a cross-chain bridge; a look at wrap BTC with ren can help put wrapped BTC routes in context. The bridge route matters because a wrapper’s backing is a separate risk from Aave.

How do you supply WBTC on Aave and earn interest?

  1. Start with WBTC in your wallet, plus enough of the network’s gas token for transactions.
  2. Open app.aave.com, connect your wallet and select a market. Each market has its own supply cap, the maximum amount it accepts.
  3. Under Assets to supply, pick WBTC. Look at the remaining cap before paying to bridge or wrap BTC: when WBTC reaches the cap, the Supply button is blocked. A deposit you cannot make can leave you with extra conversion costs.
  4. Approve WBTC spending, then confirm the supply transaction in your wallet. Approval lets Aave use the chosen amount; supplying sends WBTC into the market.
  5. Confirm success when aWBTC appears under Your supplies. Aave issues aTokens one-for-one with supplied assets, and the aWBTC balance rises as interest accrues.

Think of aWBTC like a receipt that grows with the interest earned on the WBTC behind it. The analogy stops there: it is a token in your wallet, and the actual rate depends on how much of the market borrowers use.

Supplied WBTC counts as collateral, but it carries liquidation risk only if you borrow against it. Aave yield can be low, and contract and gas costs reduce what you keep. Wrapper backing adds another risk: when RenBridge shut down, renBTC lost its redemption route. That history is a reminder to assess the wrapper separately from Aave.

Once aWBTC appears, compare its changing balance with your costs and decide whether to leave the position supplied. That gives you a practical read on whether the yield suits your holding period.

Related questions

Can WBTC be withdrawn from Aave at any time?

You can request a withdrawal while the market has enough available liquidity. Borrowing activity can affect what is available, so check the amount Aave lets you withdraw.

Is Aave interest paid in WBTC or another token?

Interest accrues in the aWBTC balance you hold. The supplied asset is WBTC, while aWBTC is Aave’s interest-bearing token.

What else should you know before supplying WBTC?

Is borrowing stablecoins against supplied WBTC safe?

It adds liquidation risk. If WBTC’s value falls against your debt, your position can become vulnerable, so borrowing needs active risk management.

How are Aave interest earnings taxed?

Tax treatment depends on where you live and your circumstances. Keep records of deposits, withdrawals and accrued interest, and check the rules that apply to you.

Which network has the cheapest gas for Aave WBTC?

Gas costs vary by network and over time. Compare the current transaction cost with the market’s WBTC availability and expected yield before moving funds.

What happens to aWBTC if WBTC loses its peg?

aWBTC represents supplied WBTC, so a loss of WBTC’s peg affects the underlying value of the position. Interest accrual does not remove that risk.
