Fairshake backs 32 House candidates as crypto bill stalls

Crypto super PAC Fairshake will back 32 House incumbents, including six with $1 million each, as the Senate’s CLARITY Act stalls ahead of November’s midterms.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomOct 9, 2026 · 06:45 UTC2 min read

Fairshake says it will support 32 House incumbents in November’s midterms, including six who will each get $1 million in campaign spending, as the crypto industry looks to keep allies in Congress after its market rules bill stalled. The Associated Press report says the group’s slate includes 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats. All backed the CLARITY Act, a bill to set federal rules for digital asset markets.

Which House candidates get the largest support?

Fairshake plans to spend $1 million each to support three Republicans and three Democrats, according to Roll Call’s report on the PAC’s plans. The Republicans are French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan. The Democrats are Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California.

Fairshake did not disclose how much it plans to spend on the other 26 candidates. The slate also includes House leaders such as Republican Majority Whip Tom Emmer of Minnesota and Democratic Caucus Chair Pete Aguilar of California.

Why is Fairshake spending now?

The CLARITY Act passed the House in July 2025 but failed to advance in the Senate in September. Lawmakers had not reached agreement on ethics rules, among other parts of the bill. Those rules would address concerns about public officials benefiting from crypto ventures.

The bill aimed to establish a federal regulatory framework for digital assets. Its failure leaves that effort unresolved, and Fairshake says all 32 House candidates it supports backed the legislation.

How does the House slate compare with Fairshake’s other spending?

The six disclosed House commitments total $6 million, but they are only part of Fairshake’s election plans. The group announced in September that it would spend nearly $30 million against Democratic Senate candidate Sherrod Brown in Ohio. Brown is challenging Republican Sen. Jon Husted.

Fairshake and its affiliated PACs reported about $120 million in cash on hand at the end of August, the AP reported. The group has not said how much more it might spend in House or Senate races before November.
