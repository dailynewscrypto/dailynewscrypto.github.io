Checking a TRC-20 USDT Transfer on Tronscan

How to check a USDT transaction on Tronscan: search by hash or address, then confirm the status, genuine token contract, recipient and amount on Tron.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 28, 2026 · 15:41 UTC2 min read

To check a USDT transfer on Tronscan, search its transaction hash or a wallet address, then confirm the status, token contract, recipient and amount. Tronscan shows the record on Tron itself, so you can compare it with what your wallet or sender reports.

How do you find the transfer?

Search by transaction hash when the sender has given you one. A hash is the transaction’s unique ID. Paste it into Tronscan’s search field and open the matching transaction. If you have no hash, search the sender’s or recipient’s Tron address and find the transfer in its transaction history.

Take a payment you are expecting. Open the matching record and read its fields: the hash identifies the transaction; Status should say “Confirmed”; From is the sending address; and To is the address that received the tokens. Compare To with your own wallet address, character by character.

Next, check the token and amount. The token should be USDT, and the amount should match what the sender says they sent. Open the token link and compare its contract address with Tether’s TRC-20 contract: TR7NHqjeKQxGTCi8q8ZY4pL8otSzgjLj6t. A token can use the USDT name without being Tether’s token, so the name alone is not enough.

Tron blocks become irreversible once 19 of 27 Super Representatives confirm them. A “Confirmed” status means the transfer is recorded on Tron; check the recipient and token details as well to see whether it reached the right place.

What if the transfer came through a bridge?

A USDT bridge moves tokens between blockchains, so a payout on Tron appears as a TRC-20 transfer from the bridge’s contract or relayer address to your Tron address. The From field may therefore show a service address rather than the sender’s wallet. The To field, token contract and amount still tell you whether the Tron payout matches what you expected.

That distinction matters when you expect USDT on a different network: a transfer on Tron confirms the Tron-side payout, while the destination network determines which token balance you should see there. Read whether to bridge USDT to Solana to understand what the destination network and token mint change for that move.

For any transfer, use the hash to inspect one specific record, or use an address to find it among several transactions. A confirmed record with the correct recipient, amount and Tether contract is the evidence that the USDT arrived at that Tron address.
