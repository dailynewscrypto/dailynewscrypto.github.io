Single-Token Liquidity on SyncSwap: What the Pool Swap Changes

Adding liquidity with one token on SyncSwap? Check pool depth, pool type, quoted LP tokens, slippage on minted LP tokens, and your exposure to both assets.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 21, 2026 · 01:22 UTC3 min read

What to check before a single-token add

Five checks cover the decision an LP needs before confirming a single-token add: pool depth, pool type, quoted LP tokens, slippage and two-asset exposure.

A single-token add swaps part of your deposit inside the pool to match its token ratio. That internal swap carries the same costs as a real swap, including price impact: the price change caused by trading against the pool’s reserves. The rest of the deposit helps form your share of both assets.

  • Compare pool depth with your deposit size. Look at the reserves of both tokens and consider how large your deposit is beside them. A large deposit in a thin pool can move the price more during the internal swap, giving you noticeable price impact. A smaller deposit relative to reserves is less likely to move the pool price as much, though the quote still matters.
  • Match the pool type to the pair. SyncSwap’s pool type affects which pool is suited to your tokens. The classic and stable pools distinction helps you match pegged assets to Stable Pool and other pairs to Classic Pool. Choosing the type that fits the pair matters because the pool’s design is intended for that kind of asset relationship.
  • Read the quoted LP tokens. The quote shows the LP tokens expected in return for your deposit. LP tokens represent a share of both pool assets, regardless of which token you deposited. Compare the quote with your intended contribution and the pool depth; the number of LP tokens is the result to assess, not a token output from a swap.
  • Set slippage tolerance with the LP quote in mind. For a single-token add, the slippage limit applies to the LP tokens minted, not to a token output. It caps how far the final LP-token amount can fall from the quote before the transaction is rejected. A stale quote can either revert or mint fewer LP tokens if the result remains within your tolerance. Recheck the quote before confirming.
  • Accept exposure to both tokens. Once you add liquidity, your pool share represents both assets. Even if you deposited just one token, price moves in the other asset now affect your position. Consider whether you are comfortable holding that two-asset exposure before you commit.

Related questions

Can I withdraw in the same single token I deposited?

Do not assume you will withdraw only the token you put in. Your LP tokens represent a share of both pool assets, so withdrawing that share relates to both. Whether you can receive just one token depends on the available withdrawal options; check what is offered before you plan around a single-token return.

Is a single-token add more expensive than a balanced one?

It can carry extra swap cost because part of the deposit is swapped inside the pool. A balanced add supplies both assets in the pool’s ratio and avoids that internal swap. The size of the difference depends on the pool’s depth and how much price impact the single-token swap causes.

Before confirming, compare the quote with the deposit you intend to make and decide whether its LP-token amount and two-asset exposure suit you. That leaves you with a clear view of the position you are about to take.
