US wins authority over $1.2B in Binance theft funds

The Justice Department won court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of Binance’s stolen crypto, part of a wider effort to freeze the funds.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 18, 2026 · 06:15 UTC2 min read

US authorities have court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a step that could help keep those funds out of the hands of the hackers. The FBI announced the action, and The Associated Press reported that the amount is part of a much larger theft.

The theft took place on or about Oct. 29. The Justice Department said North Korean military hackers it calls TraderTraitor stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from the exchange. The FBI had attributed the theft to the group. The government’s announcement does not say the full stolen amount has been recovered.

$2.7 billion frozen so far

US government agencies and private companies have helped freeze more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets, according to the Justice Department’s announcement. A freeze blocks assets from being moved while authorities work on the case. The court has authorized the government to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of that frozen total.

That distinction matters. The court authorization gives the government legal power to pursue the assets, but it does not mean a judge has made a final decision on who owns them. The AP reported that the government typically files a forfeiture claim in such cases, after which a judge decides who has a legitimate claim to the funds.

Authorities are still tracing the rest. The Justice Department said the effort to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen crypto is ongoing. It said TraderTraitor actors continue to move assets through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move tokens between blockchains; mixers obscure transaction paths.

For Binance users and the wider market, the announcement shows that law enforcement and private firms have blocked access to a sizeable share of the stolen funds. But the $1.2 billion is a portion of the more than $2.7 billion frozen, and the government has not said how much may ultimately be returned to victims.
