Send cross-chain deposits with expiry time in hand

Send a cross-chain deposit only after checking the route, fee and expiry, then leave enough time for the source chain to confirm it before the channel closes.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomSep 30, 20263 min read

To time a cross-chain deposit before expiry, check the route’s closing time, then send early enough for the source-chain transaction to be seen and confirmed. A deposit address can stop accepting new transfers even while a transaction is waiting in a wallet or mempool, the queue of unconfirmed transactions. Expiry rules differ by service, so use the time or block height shown for that specific deposit.

When should I send a cross-chain deposit?

Send once the route details are final and you have enough time left for the source chain to process the transaction. Don’t treat the address’s expiry as a target send time: a wallet may broadcast quickly, but the network still needs to include the transaction in a block, and the swap service may require further confirmations.

Check the source chain’s recent pace, especially if it is busy. A fast chain can still slow down, and a fee set too low may leave a transaction waiting. The fuller chainflip article explains how the deposit step fits into a native cross-chain swap flow. Keep the deposit amount, source asset and destination address beside you while reviewing the route; correcting those details after sending may be difficult or impossible.

What does a deposit expiry actually mean?

Expiry marks the end of the period in which a service expects a deposit for that route. Some services show a clock time, while others show a block height, the number assigned to a block on the source chain. A block-height deadline moves with that chain’s block production, so it is not always possible to convert it into an exact wall-clock time.

Expiry does not mean the swap itself has a countdown that can be extended. The route may be tied to a particular deposit address and set of instructions. A transfer sent after the channel closes may not be recognised automatically, even if the address still looks usable. Follow the service’s stated rule for whether a transaction must be broadcast, included in a block or confirmed before the deadline.

How much time should I leave for confirmation?

Work backwards from the displayed deadline and leave a buffer for both inclusion and the required confirmations. The right buffer depends on the source chain, current congestion and the service’s confirmation policy; there is no safe number of minutes that fits every deposit.

  • Read the expiry in the route details before opening your wallet.
  • Check the source chain and asset match the instructions exactly.
  • Use a fee likely to get the transaction included in time.
  • Leave room for the service to observe the required confirmations.

If there is too little time to leave that buffer, open a fresh route if the service allows it and use its new instructions. Don’t send to an old address just because it remains visible in your history.

What should I do after sending?

Save the transaction ID and watch its status on the source chain and in the swap service. A wallet’s “sent” message means it broadcast the transaction; it does not prove the service has received or accepted the deposit. If the transaction is still pending near expiry, avoid sending a second deposit unless the service tells you what to do. Two transfers can create two separate cases to resolve.

The practical rule is simple: prepare first, then send with time to spare. If the deadline is close or unclear, a new route is usually easier to track than trying to race an expiring one.
