Should You Delete Unused MetaMask Networks? Keep Funds Safe

Delete vs keep unused custom networks in MetaMask: remove one-off entries, keep networks you use or that hold funds, and restore balances with the same Chain ID.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 28, 2026 · 02:54 UTC2 min read

A cluttered MetaMask network list is easier to manage when you delete networks you no longer use; your funds stay on-chain, and you can restore the network later.

Deleting a network removes its settings from MetaMask, not the assets held at your wallet address. The network entry stores details such as its Chain ID and RPC endpoint, which tells MetaMask where to connect.

When you want a deleted network back, Chainlist can help you find the same Chain ID and add the network again. That gives you a shorter list now, with a way to restore the connection later.

Keep networks you use or that hold funds

MetaMask users should keep networks they use monthly or that hold funds, and delete one-off networks and testnets they no longer need. A shorter list makes it less likely you will pick the wrong network when sending or checking a transaction.

Before removing anything, note which networks hold balances. Deleting a network does not move or erase those funds; they remain on-chain at the same address. When you add that network again with the same Chain ID, MetaMask can show the balance again.

MetaMask also blocks deletion of the network currently selected. Built-in networks such as Ethereum Mainnet cannot be deleted. Switch to another network first; the steps below use Ethereum Mainnet as the destination.

Switch networks, then delete the unused entry

With MetaMask unlocked and your balance notes ready, remove each unused network from its options menu. The visible result after each step shows you are in the right place.

  1. Switch to Ethereum Mainnet. MetaMask shows Ethereum Mainnet as the selected network.
  2. Open the network menu. MetaMask displays the networks available in your wallet.
  3. Open the options for the unused network. Its network-specific actions appear.
  4. Choose Delete and confirm. MetaMask removes that network from the list.
  5. Review the network list and your other balances. The deleted entry is gone, and the wallet still shows its other balances; the networks you noted can be restored later.

To restore a removed network, use Chainlist to add it again with the same Chain ID. Once MetaMask connects to that network, its balance appears again at the same wallet address.

Keep a network only when you use it or it holds funds; otherwise, delete it after noting its balance status. This keeps your list tidy and helps prevent choosing the wrong network by mistake.
