Solana Token Supply Growth Can Dilute Your Share

Why did my token supply increase after I bought it? Usually, the mint authority created new tokens; Token-2022 can also raise the displayed amount while raw supply stays flat.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 21, 2026 · 08:23 UTC3 min read

A Solana token's total supply usually rises because its mint authority signed a MintTo instruction, though Token-2022 can also make the displayed amount grow while raw supply stays flat. The difference matters: newly minted tokens can reduce your share, while a display change alone does not.

An active mint authority is a detail worth including in a rugcheck. A rugcheck reviews a token for warning signs, while Solscan can show whether the authority actually minted tokens.

Why supply rises after a purchase

A token's supply rises when its mint authority signs a MintTo instruction that credits tokens to a chosen token account. The instruction adds exactly the minted amount to total supply. The new tokens may go to a team wallet, treasury, or another account; the destination appears in the transaction record.

That extra supply can shrink each holder's share even when their balance stays the same. Say you hold 10 million tokens out of a 1 billion total supply: your share is 1%. If the authority then mints another 1 billion, your 10 million becomes 0.5% of the 2 billion total.

The mint authority's power matters because it can create more tokens while it remains active. Only the current mint authority can sign MintTo instructions. Setting the mint authority to none is permanent, so no authority can mint more tokens afterward.

New tokens can also affect price if their owner sells them into a liquidity pool, where traders swap tokens. Selling adds tokens to the pool for buyers, which can push the token's price down. That is a possible market effect of a mint, not an automatic result of every supply increase.

Token-2022 offers another explanation: its interest-bearing extension can make the amount shown in a wallet or explorer grow over time without minting tokens. The displayed amount changes, but raw supply stays flat. Compare the raw supply with the UI figure before concluding that new tokens exist.

To trace a real increase, open the mint's history on Solscan and look for MintTo entries. Read each entry's amount and destination, then compare the transaction time with when you noticed the change. A rugcheck can help frame mint authority as a token risk, but the MintTo history shows whether and where new tokens were issued.

Related

Can mint authority be revoked after launch?

Yes. The current mint authority can set the authority to none, and that change is permanent. Without a mint authority, no one can sign a new MintTo instruction.

Why does circulating supply differ from total supply?

Circulating supply counts tokens considered available in the market; total supply counts all tokens issued. Tokens held in wallets that are not circulating can make the first figure lower than the second.
