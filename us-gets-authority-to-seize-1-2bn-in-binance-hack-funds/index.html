US gets authority to seize $1.2bn in Binance hack funds

US authorities won court approval to seize or restrain over $1.2 billion in Binance hack funds, part of $2.7 billion frozen since the October theft.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 15, 2026 · 06:15 UTC3 min read

The US Justice Department has court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, moving recovery efforts forward after a major exchange hack. The department announced the action on Thursday, Nov. 12, in its statement on actions against North Korean crypto theft. The FBI says North Korea was responsible.

More than $2.7 billion has been frozen

The $1.2 billion is part of more than $2.7 billion in stolen funds frozen through joint US government and private-sector efforts, according to the Justice Department. A freeze can stop assets from moving while authorities seek legal control. The department says it has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of the frozen total.

That distinction matters: court approval to seize or restrain funds does not by itself mean the money has been returned to Binance or its users. The department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are continuing. It also says the hackers are still laundering funds through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges.

The FBI links the theft to North Korea

The FBI says the theft happened on or about Oct. 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets. In an earlier FBI alert on the Binance attack, the bureau attributed it to North Korea and called the activity “TraderTraitor.” The Justice Department says members of the North Korean military carried out the theft.

The FBI also reported that the attackers were converting stolen assets mainly into Ether, or ETH. It warned that the funds could then move through decentralised finance services and instant swap services as part of further laundering. The bureau and its partners urged crypto service providers to block transactions tied to the addresses used in the laundering.

Recovery remains a legal process

The Justice Department has not said that the entire frozen amount will be forfeited or returned to customers. The seizure and restraint authorization covers only part of the funds frozen so far, while tracing and legal proceedings continue. The Associated Press report on the seizure notes that the government typically files a forfeiture claim, after which a judge determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

For Binance users, the announcement signals progress in restricting the stolen assets, but not a confirmed payout or repayment timeline. The Justice Department has not announced when the forfeiture process will conclude. Its statement says authorities are still working to track funds as the attackers move them between services.
