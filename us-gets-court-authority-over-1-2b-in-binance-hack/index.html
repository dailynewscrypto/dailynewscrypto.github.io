US gets court authority over $1.2B in Binance hack

The Justice Department has court authority over more than $1.2 billion in crypto stolen from Binance, after a $6 billion hack; tracing and recovery work continue.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomNov 17, 2026 · 08:41 UTC2 min read

U.S. authorities have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a move that could keep part of the haul out of hackers’ hands while the government works to recover it. The Justice Department’s announcement on Nov. 12 says the theft happened on or about Oct. 29 and was carried out by North Korean military members the FBI calls TraderTraitor.

The $1.2 billion is part of a larger pool. U.S. agencies and private companies have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets so far, the Justice Department said. A freeze blocks funds from being moved; the court authorization allows the department to pursue seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion within that frozen amount. The government says its efforts with crypto service providers helped stop the funds from moving beyond law enforcement’s reach.

The original theft was more than $6 billion, according to the Justice Department. The Associated Press reported that the FBI had previously attributed the attack to TraderTraitor. The amount now frozen is a substantial share of the reported loss, but it does not mean all of the stolen crypto has been recovered.

Recovery will depend on tracing the remaining funds

Authorities say the effort to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets is ongoing. The Justice Department said TraderTraitor actors continue to move funds through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. These services can help transfer or obscure the path of crypto, making it harder for investigators to follow.

For customers and other potential claimants, the court order is a step toward recovery, not a payout. The AP reported that the U.S. government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department has not said in its announcement when that process will finish or how much of the stolen assets may ultimately be returned.

The announcement also describes separate U.S. actions against North Korean cybercrime, including a scheme officials say stole more than $400 million in crypto. That figure concerns the FaceStealer scheme, not the Binance theft. The department says Binance-related tracing and recovery work continues.
