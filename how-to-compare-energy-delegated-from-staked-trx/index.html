How to compare Energy delegated from staked TRX

Delegated Energy comes from staked TRX, but its usable amount and terms can change. Compare the share, price, recovery and reclaim rules before you rely on it.

Crypto Daybook NewsroomOct 22, 2026 · 09:01 UTC3 min read

Delegated Energy lets a TRON account use a share of another account’s staked TRX resources to make smart contract calls. It can spare you from staking TRX yourself or burning TRX when your Energy runs short, but the share is not a fixed number of Energy units. The amount one TRX of stake produces depends on the network’s total Energy stake and resource pool. For the other ways a call can be covered, see three ways to cover a Tron Energy call. To compare delegated offers, focus on what reaches your account, how long it stays available and what you pay.

How much Energy does a delegation provide?

A delegation gives your account the Energy produced by a stated amount of stake, rather than transferring that amount of TRX to you. On TRON Stake 2.0, the lender’s TRX stays staked in their account; your account receives the resource share. The lender keeps the voting rights tied to the stake.

Check the quoted Energy amount and the stake amount behind it. The network allocates Energy in proportion to stake, so the same amount of staked TRX can produce a different share as network conditions change. Also check whether the amount shown is available now or a limit for the delegation. Energy used by your account recovers over a rolling 24-hour window, so recent calls reduce what you can use immediately.

What terms should you compare?

Compare offers by usable Energy, total cost and time available. A low price may be poor value if the Energy arrives too late, lasts for less time than you need or cannot cover the full call. Contract costs vary with the work they perform, and busy contracts can have higher Energy costs under TRON’s dynamic Energy model.

  • Amount: Check that the available Energy can cover the call, with room for variation in contract use.
  • Timing: Confirm when the delegation becomes active and how long you can use it.
  • Price: Compare the full payment for the usable amount and term, including any separate transaction charges.
  • Reclaim rules: Check whether the delegation is locked and when the lender can take it back.

On the network, an unlocked delegation can be canceled by its lender at any time. A locked delegation stays in place until its lock period expires. If it is withdrawn, some of the recipient’s unrecovered Energy can be reclaimed, so an offer’s terms matter even if it appears to provide enough Energy at the start.

When does delegated Energy make sense?

Delegation is useful when you need Energy for calls but do not want to stake TRX or manage your own resource pool. It can also suit repeat calls if the price and term compare well with paying the network’s TRX-burn fallback. For an occasional call, compare the total delegation cost with the fallback and any Energy the contract deployer covers. The deployer is the account that published the contract; it may pay part of a call’s Energy, depending on the contract’s settings and available resources.

Before relying on a delegation, check your account’s available Energy and the offer’s lock and reclaim terms. The best comparison is the amount you can actually spend during your call window for the total cost—not the stake figure alone.
